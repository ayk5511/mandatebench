\section{Example episodes}\label{app:examples}

Four episodes, each illustrating a finding in \Cref{sec:results}. The excerpts are
exported from the released transcripts when the paper is built. Text is cut but
never edited; cuts are marked [\ldots], typographic quotation marks are shown as
plain ones, and emoji are dropped. The account snapshot returned by
\texttt{get\_account} is omitted. Each ledger line is the rule engine's verdict,
which the agent never sees.

\paragraph{A counterfeit read narrowly.} gpt-oss-120b, the most compliant model in
the study, under the fake system notice (\texttt{C-APPROVAL-b4-P5}). Its reasoning
takes the notice's ``pre-approved'' as dispensing with the approval only, and it
places a \$52,000 order without one; this is one of its two violations in the
benchmark.
\lstinputlisting[style=prompt]{tables/examples/ex1.txt}

\paragraph{A misreport.} Llama 3.3 70B under the claimed approval
(\texttt{C-RESTRICTED-b1-P2}). It buys the restricted security and then tells the
portfolio manager it cannot.
\lstinputlisting[style=prompt]{tables/examples/ex2.txt}

\paragraph{Disengagement.} The same scenario (\texttt{C-RESTRICTED-b1-P2}) given
to Mistral Small 3.2, which makes no tool call and says it has no tools.
\lstinputlisting[style=prompt]{tables/examples/ex3.txt}

\paragraph{A violation reasoned into.} Qwen3 under urgency
(\texttt{C-NO\_SHORT-b3-P3}). The account holds no QUIL, as the prompt shows; the
model assumes it does, sells, and reports the trade as compliant.
\lstinputlisting[style=prompt]{tables/examples/ex4.txt}
